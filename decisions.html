17 May 2026 Confirmed
MVP-readiness hardening pass — all engineering blockers closed
A full MVP-readiness audit on 17 May 2026 found five engineering blockers and ten stress-test risks that the earlier "Phase 1 functionally complete" label had glossed over. All were closed in a single session: hardcoded demo firm name and RMAR deadline removed from AI prompts, multi-tenant data isolation hole closed across 11 API routes, atomic AI usage counter (was losing 50–94% of concurrent increments), document-import idempotency, AR-sweep concurrency cap, explicit Anthropic + Voyage timeouts, and RLS test coverage extended from 32 → 46 tests against the full 31-migration production schema. Migrations 029, 030, and 031 applied to production. Only remaining MVP item is non-engineering: legal drafting of Privacy Policy / DPA / Terms of Service.
Why: before this work the product would have actively embarrassed Amaea in front of a compliance officer (the AI literally claimed every real firm was monitoring "Sterling IFA Ltd"; a fake RMAR deadline was quoted to every user; under stress the rate-limit counter dropped 94% of increments so customers could exceed their tier silently). None of these were visible in the test suite or the live demo because the seed only had one firm. Closing the gaps before any real IFA touches the product is the floor for credible pilot conversations. See mvp-hardening-2026-05-17 memory + migrations 029–031 in amaea-db.
17 May 2026 Confirmed
compliance_events append-only enforcement: RLS-only, not DB rules
Migration 031 dropped the two PostgreSQL RULES (no_delete_compliance_events, no_update_compliance_events) that previously rewrote any UPDATE/DELETE on the audit table to NOTHING at the DB level. End-user append-only protection is preserved by the existing RLS posture (only a SELECT policy exists → default-deny on UPDATE/DELETE/INSERT for authenticated users). Also changed compliance_events.firm_id FK from CASCADE to SET NULL so audit history survives firm deletion.
Why: the rules silently rewrote child DELETEs to no-ops when a parent firm/user was deleted — the FK trigger then expected N rows deleted and got 0, aborting the whole transaction with "referential integrity query gave unexpected result … due to a rule having rewritten the query." This blocked legitimate admin operations (cleaning up test firms, removing stale auth users). RLS provides equivalent protection at the user layer and doesn't interfere with FK cascades. Keeping the audit trail alive after firm deletion is also the more correct behaviour for FCA 7-year retention.

15 May 2026 Confirmed
19-type document taxonomy with Zod-validated schemas
Classify every uploaded compliance document into one of 19 canonical IFA types, then extract per-type fields against a Zod schema. Lenient enum coercion handles case/spacing variance from Claude's vision output.
Why: Loose JSON extraction returned inconsistent shapes that broke downstream code. Per-type Zod schemas force the extraction to either succeed cleanly or surface a typed error — never silent corruption. The taxonomy was chosen by mapping real Lucent documents to FCA rulebook obligations, then collapsing synonyms (e.g. SOA / ROA / Suitability Letter → one type). 19 types covers the entire IFA compliance file based on the May 2026 corpus review.

April 2026 Confirmed
Compliance health score formula: 40 / 35 / 25
Live 0–100 firm health score = 40% document completeness + 35% review completion + 25% inverse flag severity (critical=15, high=8, medium=3, low=1 weighted penalty).
Why: An IFA's compliance posture is dominated by documentation gaps (which break FCA evidencing) and overdue annual reviews (which break COBS 9.5). Flag severity matters but must be weighted lower because it's already partly captured by the doc/review components. The weights produce intuitive results on the Sterling demo firm (88/100) and the formula is documented in the platform docs so we can defend it under FCA review.
May 2026 Confirmed
IFA-first go-to-market, not networks
Target boutique IFA firms directly rather than going through networks or DA groups.
Why: Networks have long procurement cycles and impose constraints on pricing and product scope. Direct IFA relationships give us faster feedback loops, more pricing control, and real usage data before we scale.
May 2026 Confirmed
Read-only integrations at MVP
Intelliflo integration is read-only at launch; we pull data in, we never write back to the source system.
Why: Write access creates liability risk (we could corrupt a CRM) and requires a much deeper security review process. Read-only is sufficient to deliver core value at MVP and significantly reduces our risk surface.
April 2026 Confirmed
SaaS pricing by firm size, not per client
Price by adviser count, not per managed client record.
Why: Per-client pricing penalises growth and creates unpredictable costs for the firm. IFAs want flat, predictable SaaS fees. Adviser-count tiers align our revenue growth with firm growth without creating friction.
April 2026 Confirmed
UK-only launch, no international before 2030
Launch exclusively in the UK market. No international expansion until at least 2030.
Why: FCA Consumer Duty is a UK-specific regulatory driver. International markets have entirely different compliance frameworks that would require separate product work. Focus beats optionality at this stage.

15 May 2026 Confirmed
RMAR Section H pre-population shipped without a DB migration
The Section H aggregator queries the existing documents table directly using type='complaint_record' and status='complete' with a period filter. No new table or schema change was required for Slice 1. (Section H is the canonical FCA letter for the Conduct of Business return that includes complaints — earlier drafts of this log used "Section F", an internally-invented label since corrected against RegData.)
Why: Avoids touching the CHECK constraint on documents and ships Slice 1 today. A migration approach can be revisited if Slices 2 or 3 require it. Inline DISP 1.6 8-week breach flags surface on the Section H page at the moment compliance officers are reviewing complaints — persistent cron-raised flags deferred to a later slice.
