1. Executive Summary #

The UK mortgage intermediary sector is the most natural first adjacency for Amaea after IFA. It is large (approximately 5,000 firms, ~34,000 active advisers), growing in intermediary share (87% of all mortgages in 2024, forecast 91% by 2026), and under increasing FCA compliance pressure that maps directly to Amaea's core capabilities.

Market size: Gross mortgage lending was £237.5bn in 2024, with the intermediary-routed portion representing ~£207bn. Reported sector revenue from mortgage broking fell slightly to ~£1.37bn in 2023 (2024 data pending, FCA RMAR). The broker market itself is valued at approximately USD 3.16bn in 2025, growing at ~6.5% CAGR to 2031.

Regulatory driver: The FCA's January 2025 Dear CEO letter to mortgage intermediaries named Consumer Duty embedding, suitability of advice, financial promotions, high-pressure selling cultures, conflicts of interest in appointed representative (AR) models, and vulnerability identification as the watchpoints for the next two years. A March 2026 thematic review into second charge mortgages found systematic suitability failures, record-keeping gaps, and inadequate vulnerability processes — confirming that compliance quality in this sector lags the bar Consumer Duty sets.

Fit with Amaea: Strong. The compliance obligations (Consumer Duty / PRIN 2A, suitability documentation under MCOB 4, vulnerable customer identification, T&C scheme administration, file reviews, outcome MI) are structurally similar to the IFA obligations Amaea is already building for. The main difference is the governing sourcebook: MCOB rather than COBS. Key workflow differences (mortgage fact-finds vs. suitability letters, ESIS vs. suitability reports) are material but learnable from a single experienced hire.

Key risks:

  • Curvestone AI + OMS already deploying AI compliance checking in mortgage CRM workflows as of April 2026 — Amaea enters a market where an early mover exists.
  • Network distribution dominates: selling to the DA (directly authorised) tail of 3,000+ small firms is a different GTM motion than selling to a network's central compliance team.
  • AI regulatory risk: FCA is actively watching how AI is used in advice and compliance decisions.

Recommended launch posture: Base case — Mortgage Founders Programme launch at approximately M53 (September 2030). Amaea should use 2027–2030 to build two things quietly: (a) one advisory relationship with a mid-size DA mortgage firm to test product-market fit and shape the roadmap, and (b) one domain expert hire or formal adviser from a network compliance background. Do not build mortgage-specific features before IFA product is validated.

2. Regulatory Landscape #

2.1 FCA as Regulator #

The Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) is the sole conduct regulator for UK mortgage intermediaries. Firms must either be directly authorised (DA) by the FCA or operate as appointed representatives (ARs) of an authorised principal. There is no dual-regulator structure (unlike banks, which also face PRA oversight). The FCA's mortgage regulatory priorities have historically been published via portfolio letters; from 2026 the FCA replaced these with annual Regulatory Priorities reports by sector.

Approximately 5,000 mortgage intermediary firms are registered with the FCA. Nearly one in ten new firms joining the register in H2 2025 offered mortgage advice (316 new entrants from 3,331 new registrants in that period). The FCA Register held 69,385 active firms across all regulated sectors during H2 2025.

Relevant FCA register and RMAR data:

  • 34,149 registered mortgage adviser posts in 2024 (down from 36,836 in 2023 — a 7.3% fall, attributed to market contraction in 2023–2024)
  • ~12,000 intermediary firms submit RMAR returns annually across mortgage, insurance, and investment mediation
  • Equity release: approximately 6,200 advisers with this separate permission

2.2 MCOB — Mortgages and Home Finance: Conduct of Business Sourcebook #

MCOB is the primary rulebook for mortgage intermediaries. Its purpose: promote fairness, prevent consumer harm, encourage competition. It covers all regulated mortgage contracts, home purchase plans, home reversion plans, lifetime mortgages, and sale and rent back agreements.

Key chapters relevant to compliance software:

MCOB Chapter Subject Compliance Software Relevance
MCOB 3A Financial promotions Approval workflow, fair/clear/not misleading checks
MCOB 4 Advice and selling standards Fact-find adequacy, suitability assessment, advised vs. execution-only
MCOB 4.7A Suitability for advised sales Core of every file review — the suitability assessment obligation
MCOB 6 Pre-offer disclosure (ESIS) Document generation, delivery tracking
MCOB 11 Responsible lending Affordability data collection, income verification
MCOB Sch 1 Record-keeping requirements All records must be available within 2 business days of FCA request

MCOB 4.7A suitability — what it requires: Firms giving advised mortgage sales must take reasonable steps to ensure the contract is suitable for the customer. Factors assessed include: whether requirements fall within the lender's known eligibility criteria; whether interest-only, repayment, or combination is appropriate; appropriate term; and documentation of the reasoning. This maps directly to the kind of structured, auditable fact-find workflow Amaea would build.

MCOB record-keeping: Records must be retained for a minimum period (typically 3 years from the advice date for mortgage records; 5 years where applicable under MiFID-equivalent standards for lifetime mortgages and equity release) and must be producible within 2 business days on FCA request. This creates a strong pull toward structured digital record systems.

2025 Mortgage Rule Review (PS25/11): On 22 July 2025, the FCA published PS25/11 simplifying certain MCOB rules. Key changes: removal of the "interaction trigger" (which previously forced advice every time a firm contacted a customer about a mortgage); removal of mandatory full affordability assessment when a term is only being reduced. The changes were contested by intermediaries and their trade associations, who feared lenders would use the permissive rules to disintermediate brokers. Impact on compliance workflows: modest — suitability documentation obligations for advised sales remain unchanged.

2.3 PERG — Perimeter Guidance on Mortgage Activities #

PERG Chapter 4 governs which mortgage-related activities require FCA authorisation. The key points for Amaea's purposes:

  • Advising on regulated mortgage contracts is a regulated activity only when it relates to a specific contract (generic financial education is not regulated).
  • Six regulated mortgage activities in total require authorisation or exemption.
  • Arranging (bringing about) and making arrangements for mortgages are separate regulated activities.
  • A firm providing generic information without identifying a specific product does not need authorisation, but if generic advice leads to a specific recommendation, the whole interaction is regulated.

This matters for Amaea's product design: any AI-generated suggestion about a specific product for a specific client would fall within the regulatory perimeter and require clear human adviser sign-off.

2.4 Consumer Duty — PRIN 2A Applied to Mortgage Advice #

Consumer Duty (in force July 2023) applies in full to mortgage intermediaries. The four outcome areas — products and services, price and value, consumer understanding, consumer support — must all be actively evidenced.

Key obligations for mortgage intermediaries under Consumer Duty:

  • Products and services: Intermediaries must assess whether the products they recommend meet the needs, characteristics, and objectives of the target market. Where they are acting as a distributor (not manufacturer), they must obtain relevant product information from the lender.
  • Price and value: Firms must be able to demonstrate fair value — that the fee or commission received is proportionate to the benefit delivered. The FCA named this as a priority area in the January 2025 Dear CEO letter, flagging conflicts of interest where advisers are financially incentivised to recommend products carrying higher commission.
  • Consumer understanding: Communications must enable customers to evaluate their options by assessing benefits, risks, and costs. Financial promotions must avoid exploiting behavioural biases. The FCA expects testing, monitoring, and adaptation of communications.
  • Consumer support: Support must be designed to meet the needs of retail customers, including those with characteristics of vulnerability (PRIN 2A.6.2R).

Outcomes monitoring obligation: Firms must regularly monitor and test outcomes across their customer base, including identifying whether any group of customers (e.g., those with vulnerability characteristics) receives materially different outcomes. The FCA found in its 2024–2025 multi-firm reviews that MI on outcomes was often inadequate — lacking breadth, depth, and regular review cadence.

2.5 SM&CR for Mortgage Firms #

The Senior Managers and Certification Regime was extended to FCA solo-regulated firms (including mortgage intermediaries) in December 2019.

Mortgage broker firms typically fall in the Core tier of SM&CR unless they meet Enhanced firm thresholds. Key obligations:

  • Every Senior Management Function (SMF) holder must have a Statement of Responsibilities.
  • SMF holders must be assessed as fit and proper at least annually.
  • Six prescribed responsibilities for Core firms.
  • Certification functions (e.g., customer-facing advisers) must be certified annually.

2026 reforms (PS26/6): The FCA finalised Phase 1 SM&CR reforms in April 2026, effective in two stages (24 April 2026 and 10 July 2026). Changes include extending validity of criminal record checks for SMF applications from 3 to 6 months, removing duplicate checks within the same firm/group, and new fit-and-proper guidance. These are administrative simplifications and do not alter the substance of oversight obligations.

Relevance to Amaea: Amaea should build functionality for annual F&P attestation tracking, SoR management, and SMF register maintenance — these are standard across both IFA and mortgage firm compliance programmes.

2.6 Recent Enforcement Actions and FCA Communications #

January 2025 — FCA Dear CEO Letter to Mortgage Intermediaries The most significant recent regulatory signal. Key supervisory priorities stated for the following two years:

  1. Consumer Duty embedding: The FCA's primary focus. Firms must demonstrate that customers' needs and circumstances are at the heart of the advice process. The FCA will review whether sufficient assessment of customer circumstances is being carried out and whether advice given is suitable.
  2. High-pressure selling: Supervisory work had already found firms with cultures driven by sales targets, with advisers financially incentivised to recommend higher-commission products. The FCA expects incentive schemes to not reward quantity over quality.
  3. Fair value assessment: Firms must demonstrate that their products and services offer fair value.
  4. Financial promotions: Firms must not exploit consumers' behavioural biases. Communications must avoid foreseeable harm.
  5. Conflicts of interest and conditional selling: Estate agents requiring buyers to use specific brokers (conditional selling) named as an ongoing concern. The FCA is reviewing incentive structures across the chain.
  6. Appointed representative management: The FCA flagged the retention of dormant ARs, mismanagement of conflicts within AR networks, and incorrect registration of trading names as ongoing failures.

CEOs were expected to discuss the letter with their leadership team and satisfy themselves that these areas are addressed under existing Consumer Duty programmes.

March 2026 — FCA Second Charge Mortgage Thematic Review The FCA reviewed a sample of second charge firms covering over 40% of market share. Findings included:

  • Advisers were assessing eligibility for a loan rather than suitability — conflating the two, which the FCA characterised as a conduct failure.
  • Affordability assessments overlooked key living expenses (childcare, household goods/repairs).
  • Some advisers recommended debt consolidation when it was not clearly affordable or appropriate.
  • Advisers failed to pass full expenditure details to lenders, causing lenders to underestimate client costs.
  • Record keeping was incomplete and inconsistent, making it impossible to retrospectively assess advice quality.
  • Fee transparency was poor — fees were often added to loans without clear disclosure.

This review's findings matter beyond second charge: the FCA explicitly said the findings inform expectations for the wider intermediary market.

FCA 2026 Mortgage Regulatory Priorities Report Replacing portfolio letters, this annual document (published March 2026) confirmed: evidence-based Consumer Duty outcomes monitoring; reviewing suitability of advice for debt consolidation and later-life borrowing; monitoring conflicts of interest; and addressing standards raised by the second charge review.

2.7 Vulnerable Customer Obligations #

The FCA's Consumer Duty (PRIN 2A.6.2R) requires firms to design and deliver support that meets the needs of customers with characteristics of vulnerability. This is not a bolt-on — it must be embedded in the advice process.

For mortgage brokers specifically, vulnerability characteristics relevant to the advice process include: mental health issues affecting decision-making capacity; recent life events (bereavement, job loss, divorce); limited financial resilience; and low financial literacy. Advisers must:

  • Identify vulnerability indicators during the fact-find.
  • Document what adaptations to the advice process were made.
  • Evidence that the customer understood the product and its risks.
  • Capture this in a way that can be reviewed and reported as part of outcomes MI.

The FCA's March 2025 multi-firm review on retail banks found that firms generally lacked breadth and detail in their vulnerability MI, with data not consistently giving a clear picture of outcomes for vulnerable customers. The same gap is likely present across the intermediary market.

Amaea opportunity: A structured vulnerability screening and documentation workflow — comparable to what Amaea is building for IFAs — has clear demand in the mortgage broker market.

2.8 Anti-Money Laundering — MLR 2017 #

Mortgage brokers are not always within the scope of the Money Laundering Regulations 2017 (MLR 2017) in the same way that estate agents and conveyancers are. However, where mortgage brokers also carry on activities as credit brokers or act in arranging finance for clients, CDD obligations can apply. More importantly, the FCA's AML requirements under SYSC and the Money Laundering Sourcebook (AML section of SYSC) apply to all FCA-authorised firms.

In practice, most mortgage intermediaries carry out Customer Due Diligence (CDD) on their clients, often using third-party ID verification tools (SmartSearch, Credas, Thirdfort, etc.) because:

  1. Lenders require evidence of identity verification before submitting applications.
  2. Network/principal firm compliance requirements mandate CDD as part of the T&C scheme.
  3. The FCA expects AML controls proportionate to risk across all regulated firms.

CDD framework (MLR 2017):

  • Standard CDD: Identity verification and beneficial ownership for all business relationships.
  • Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD): Mandatory for PEPs, high-risk jurisdictions, complex/unusual transactions.
  • Record retention: 5 years from end of relationship.
  • Firms must take a risk-based approach and maintain policies, controls, and procedures.

Amaea consideration: The AML/KYC workflow is currently served by specialist point solutions (SmartSearch acquired Credas in October 2025, serving 7,500+ regulated businesses). Amaea's angle is not AML verification per se but evidencing that the CDD process was completed and documented within the client file — a workflow and audit trail play rather than a verification play.

4. Customer Segmentation #

4.1 Networks (Appointed Representative Principals) #

Networks are the largest distribution channel in the mortgage intermediary market. They hold the FCA authorisation; their appointed representatives (ARs) operate under the network's umbrella. Networks provide: FCA authorisation, Professional Indemnity (PI) insurance, compliance oversight (T&C scheme), technology (sourcing systems, CRM, often compliance tools), and lender panel access.

The network's central compliance team carries the regulatory responsibility for all its ARs. This means networks have both the pain (managing compliance for hundreds or thousands of advisers) and the power (they can mandate tools to their entire AR base). A network sale is high ACV but requires a different sales motion — procurement, compliance director approval, integration with existing tech stack.

4.3 Specialist Segments #

Buy-to-Let (BTL): A large specialist segment. BTL gross lending reached £33.2bn in 2024, up 10% on 2023. Complex compliance: landlord-specific fact-finds, rental income stress testing, portfolio landlord assessments (PRA rules), and often different lender eligibility criteria. Compliance documentation is more complex than standard residential.

Equity Release: ~6,200 advisers hold equity release qualifications (CF8 / CeRER). Separate regulatory sub-set with lifetime mortgage rules under MCOB. Equity Release Council (ERC) membership brings additional conduct standards. Vulnerability is particularly acute in this segment given the age of customers.

Second Charge / Secured Loans: Smaller market but under intense FCA scrutiny following the March 2026 thematic review (see Section 2.6). Firms must be separately authorised for second charge business.

Bridging and Commercial: Growing segment. 68% of brokers expected more bridging business in 2024. Less standardised processes; commercial lending is often unregulated or regulated only at the FCA margin. Compliance documentation needs vary significantly.

Sharia / Islamic Finance: Niche but growing. Home purchase plans (HPPs) are regulated under MCOB alongside conventional mortgages. Advisers need specific training and product knowledge. Compliance documentation mirrors conventional but with HPP-specific terminology.

6. Compliance Pain Points #

What mortgage broker compliance officers actually struggle with, and where Amaea can add value.

6.1 Suitability Documentation and Fact-Find Quality #

The single largest operational pain. MCOB 4.7A requires the firm to take "reasonable steps" to ensure the mortgage is suitable. What this means in practice: a structured fact-find capturing income, expenditure, credit history, existing debt, repayment vehicle, term preference, vulnerability indicators, and the client's stated objectives.

The gap in most firms: fact-finds are partially completed, inconsistently structured across advisers, stored in formats that make post-hoc review difficult, and not systematically checked before the case goes to the lender or compliance. The FCA's second charge review (March 2026) found that expenditure items (childcare, household goods) were routinely missed. This is a documentation and workflow problem, not an adviser knowledge problem.

Amaea's AI opportunity: Pre-submission completeness checking that flags missing data fields, cross-references stated income against typical expenditure patterns for the client's profile, and surfaces suitability gaps before the file reaches the compliance officer. Curvestone AI is already doing this inside OMS (April 2026) — Amaea needs to understand the positioning difference clearly.

6.2 File Review — Manual, Slow, and Inconsistent #

Compliance officers and T&C supervisors in networks and larger DA firms conduct file reviews — checking a sample of completed mortgage cases against MCOB and Consumer Duty standards. In most firms, this is done manually: pulling the file, reading through the fact-find and suitability documentation, checking against a checklist, documenting outcomes, and reporting findings upward.

This process is time-consuming (30–90 minutes per file), inconsistent (different reviewers apply different standards), and provides only sample coverage (typically 10–25% of cases, leaving the remainder unchecked). Consumer Duty's outcomes monitoring obligation requires systematic evidence of good outcomes across the entire client base — not a sample.

Amaea's AI opportunity: Automated first-pass file review using AI to check every case against a configurable ruleset, surfacing exceptions for human review. This converts a sampling process into a 100% coverage process. The firm's compliance officer then focuses on the exceptions the AI flags, not on reading every routine case.

6.3 Vulnerability Identification and Documentation #

The FCA's Dear CEO letter (January 2025) called out vulnerability identification as a specific priority. Vulnerability must be identified during the fact-find, documented in the file, adapted into the advice process, and reported as part of outcomes MI.

Most firms have a vulnerability screening question or section in their fact-find, but the documentation of what was done with that information — how the advice process was adapted, what extra support was offered, whether the client confirmed understanding — is often absent or formulaic. The FCA expects evidence, not a checkbox.

Amaea's AI opportunity: Guided vulnerability workflow prompts (similar to what Amaea builds for IFAs), structured documentation capture, and inclusion of vulnerability data in outcomes MI dashboards.

6.4 Training and Competency (T&C) Scheme Administration #

Under SM&CR and the FCA's training and competency sourcebook (TC), firms must maintain a T&C scheme for their advisers. In networks, the network's central compliance team manages this and provides evidence to the FCA on request. In DA firms, the responsibility falls on the Principal / Head of Compliance.

T&C administration involves: maintaining records of qualifications and Continuing Professional Development (CPD); tracking supervised practice periods for new advisers; annual competency assessments; and documenting remedial actions where competency gaps are found. Across a large network with hundreds of ARs, this is a substantial administrative burden.

Amaea's opportunity: A structured T&C register within the compliance platform — tracking CPD, qualifications, supervision status, and annual assessments.

6.5 Management Information (MI) to Network / Principal #

ARs must submit regular MI to their network / principal as part of the T&C scheme and Consumer Duty outcomes monitoring. This includes: case volumes; complaint data; file review outcomes; training completion; and vulnerability MI. Most firms assemble this manually from spreadsheets and CRM reports, creating reporting lag and inconsistency.

Networks have their own MI portals (often bespoke or built into their internal compliance tools), but the data quality that comes in from ARs is variable.

Amaea's opportunity: Automated MI pack generation — pre-formatted for network reporting requirements, pulling directly from the compliance system.

6.6 Consumer Duty Outcomes Monitoring #

The Consumer Duty requires firms to demonstrate — with evidence — that customers are receiving good outcomes across the four outcome areas. For mortgage intermediaries, this means tracking: complaint rates by product type; reasons for non-advised (execution-only) sales; re-mortgage rates and outcomes for customers who took a particular recommendation; vulnerability identification rates and adaptations made; and fee-to-benefit ratio across the book.

Most firms are doing this inadequately. The FCA's multi-firm reviews have consistently found that MI lacks breadth and detail. This is partly a data problem (the CRM doesn't capture enough) and partly a process problem (no one is responsible for compiling and reviewing the MI systematically).

Amaea's core value proposition: A structured outcomes monitoring dashboard that aggregates compliance and client data, surfaces trends and anomalies, and generates a quarterly Consumer Duty outcomes report ready for the board or the network.

6.7 Financial Promotions Approval #

MCOB 3A requires that financial promotions are fair, clear, and not misleading. In practice, mortgage brokers produce a lot of marketing content — website copy, social media posts, rate comparison posts, email campaigns. Each piece needs to be reviewed and approved by a person with the appropriate competency.

This is often done informally (the adviser posts something; nobody checks it). The FCA has flagged this as an enforcement risk area.

Amaea's opportunity: A financial promotions approval workflow — submission, review, approval sign-off, audit trail. Lower priority than file review and outcomes monitoring but a clear module add-on.

6.8 Appointed Representative Oversight (for Networks and Principal Firms) #

The January 2025 Dear CEO letter specifically called out failures in AR oversight: retention of dormant ARs, conflicts of interest within AR networks, incorrect registration of trading names. The FCA holds the principal firm responsible for the conduct of all its ARs.

For network compliance teams, the challenge is maintaining visibility across hundreds of AR firms — monitoring their case files, competency records, consumer outcomes data, and complaint patterns — without an army of compliance officers.

Amaea's opportunity: A network-level compliance oversight dashboard — a principal firm's view across all ARs, surfacing outliers and enabling targeted intervention without proportionate manual effort.

8.2 Amaea Mortgage Pricing — Sanity Check #

The internal model has Mortgage tier at 70% of IFA pricing:

Tier IFA Price Proposed Mortgage Price (70%) Sanity Check
Essentials £699/mo £489/mo Reasonable for 1–3 adviser DA firm; comparable to compliance consultancy retainer
Professional £1,599/mo £1,119/mo Right for 4–15 adviser DA firm or AR-heavy network member; needs to displace part of consultancy spend
Scale £2,199/mo £1,539/mo Mid-size DA firm (15–50 advisers); plausible if it displaces a compliance consultant
Enterprise £3,500+/mo £2,450+/mo Network central compliance team; needs to demonstrate ROI across hundreds of ARs

Assessment: The 70% discount to IFA pricing is defensible given:

  1. Average mortgage broker firm is smaller than average IFA firm.
  2. Revenue per case is lower (proc fees typically £300–800 per mortgage vs. advice fees of £1,500–3,000 for investment/pension advice).
  3. Compliance tooling spend is lower as a percentage of revenue in the mortgage market.

The Essentials tier at £489/mo is at the top of what a 1–2 adviser DA firm would consider without a strong ROI story. To land this tier, Amaea needs to demonstrate it replaces at least one element of existing spend (e.g., the compliance consultant retainer, or the manual file review time).

The Professional and Scale tiers are the best fit for 5–50 adviser DA firms — the sweet spot of the market. Founders Programme discount at 20% (bringing Professional to ~£895/mo) makes the entry point very competitive against a compliance consultancy retainer.

Recommendation: Keep the 70% ratio. Consider whether the Essentials tier needs a further simplification (fewer modules) to justify the £489 price point for the smallest brokers, rather than discounting the price.

12. Risks and Unknowns #

These are the things Amaea does not yet know well enough that could materially affect the mortgage sector strategy.

12.1 Curvestone AI / OMS Competitive Risk #

Risk: OMS + Curvestone AI launched in April 2026 — four years before Amaea's target mortgage launch. By 2030, if this product achieves meaningful penetration among mortgage brokers, the "automated compliance checking" use case may be commoditised. If OMS (the case management workflow) becomes the default system with compliance baked in, Amaea may struggle to sell a separate compliance platform.

What we don't know: Curvestone's pricing, adoption rate, whether networks are mandating it, and whether it is expanding beyond file completeness checking into outcomes monitoring and MI. This needs active monitoring.

Mitigation: Amaea's value proposition is broader than file checking. The outcomes monitoring, T&C, and longitudinal MI dimensions are less competed. But the team must track OMS/Curvestone quarterly and be prepared to pivot positioning.

12.2 Network Mandated Tools #

Risk: If major networks (Primis, MAB, Stonebridge) mandate their own internal compliance tools or a single preferred technology partner for their ARs, Amaea's access to the AR market is significantly constrained.

What we don't know: Which networks are currently building or procuring centralised compliance tools, and what the purchasing timeline looks like.

Mitigation: Amaea should attempt to become the preferred tool for a network, not compete against a network. A partnership with one mid-size network (e.g., The Right Mortgage) as a Founders Programme design partner would be the ideal distribution strategy.

12.3 Market Contraction / Structural Change #

Risk: The mortgage market contracted sharply in 2022–2024 due to interest rate rises. If rates remain elevated or another shock occurs, broker numbers and revenue decline again, squeezing willingness to spend on compliance tools.

What we don't know: What interest rate environment will prevail in 2029–2030 when Amaea launches.

Mitigation: Compliance spend is less cyclical than broker revenue — regulatory obligations don't reduce in a downturn. But the ability to pay for premium SaaS does decline. If the market contracts, Amaea may need to sharpen the ROI story (compliance tooling that reduces staff time, avoiding enforcement costs) rather than leading with features.

12.4 FCA Regulatory Direction on AI in Compliance #

Risk: The FCA is actively watching AI use in regulated activities. If the FCA determines that AI-generated compliance assessments require specific regulatory frameworks, human-in-the-loop mandates, or liability attribution that constrains what Amaea can automate, the product roadmap needs to adapt.

What we don't know: The FCA's 2026–2028 AI strategy as applied to compliance tools (as opposed to advice tools). Curvestone AI claims to have engaged with the FCA's innovation programme — Amaea should understand the regulatory position before building mortgage-specific AI features.

Mitigation: Amaea's AI capabilities in IFA will have been tested and positioned by 2029. The learnings transfer. Amaea should position AI as "surfacing exceptions for human review" rather than "making compliance decisions" — this is the safe and accurate framing given the FCA's current signals.

12.5 Pricing Sensitivity in the Mortgage Market #

Risk: Mortgage broker firms are generally smaller and less profitable per firm than IFA firms. A compliance SaaS tool at £489–£1,539/month may be too expensive for the long tail of 1–5 adviser DA firms.

What we don't know: The actual willingness-to-pay of DA mortgage firms for a compliance SaaS platform, and how price-sensitive they are relative to IFA firms.

Mitigation: The Founders Programme design should include deep discovery interviews with 20+ DA mortgage firms before committing to pricing. The 70% of IFA pricing assumption is a starting hypothesis, not a validated figure.

12.6 MCOB Familiarity Gap #

Risk: Amaea's founding team has IFA/COBS expertise. MCOB is a different rulebook with different terminology, different fact-find requirements, different product structures, and different lender relationships. Building MCOB-compliant compliance workflows without deep domain knowledge creates product risk.

What we don't know: How long it will take to build genuine MCOB expertise internally, and whether the one advisory hire planned is sufficient or whether a more permanent senior compliance hire is needed.

Mitigation: This is the primary reason to begin building the advisory relationship in 2028 rather than closer to launch. Two years of informed product development — with a MCOB expert reviewing the roadmap quarterly — is the minimum viable preparation.
